B1 — Blocker

Connect a payment provider

This is the whole game. Everything else on this page is a refinement of a store that cannot currently take a rupee.

Choosing one

Shopify Payments is not available to Indian merchants, so you need a third-party gateway. Any of these work with Shopify Basic:

ProviderTypical rateNotes
Razorpay2% + GSTMost common on Indian Shopify stores. UPI, cards, netbanking, wallets. Supports COD via a separate app
Cashfree~1.75–2%Similar coverage, sometimes faster settlement
PayU~2%Long-established, heavier onboarding

The plan assumed Razorpay, and nothing found here argues against it.

Steps

1. Open a Razorpay account

You will need: PAN, GSTIN, bank account in the business name, cancelled cheque or bank statement, and address proof. Use the same legal entity name as your bank account — a mismatch is the single most common cause of KYC rejection.

2. Wait for activation

Usually 2–4 working days. You can complete everything else on this page meanwhile.

3. Connect it in Shopify

Settings → Payments → Add payment method → Third-party providers → Razorpay. Paste the Key ID and Key Secret from the Razorpay dashboard.

4. Decide on Cash on Delivery

COD is a large share of Indian furniture orders, and also the source of most returns and refused deliveries. If you want it: Settings → Payments → Manual payment methods → Cash on Delivery. Consider charging a small COD fee, or restricting it below a rupee threshold, so a refused ₹19,999 desk does not cost you the round trip.

Do not skip the live test

A connected gateway is not a working gateway. Section S4 is how you find out whether money actually lands in your account.

B2 — Blocker

Write the three missing policies

Checked against the live store:

PolicyStatusNeeded because
Privacy PolicyExists—
Refund / CancellationMissingRazorpay requires it to approve you. Indian e-commerce rules require it
Terms of ServiceMissingRazorpay requires it. Defines what you are actually selling
Shipping PolicyMissingRazorpay requires it. Sets delivery expectations before a dispute, not after

Settings → Policies. Shopify offers a template for each — start there and edit, rather than writing from nothing.

What yours specifically need to say

  • Refund window and who pays return freight. For furniture this is the expensive clause. A 20 kg chair returned across India at your cost can erase the margin on several orders. State it plainly.
  • Assembled or damaged-in-transit goods. Say what happens when a desk arrives with a scratched top — replacement part, full swap, or partial refund.
  • Delivery window by region. Delhi NCR versus rest of India. Vague promises become chargebacks.
  • Warranty. Duration and what it excludes. This is also the answer to the most common pre-sale question, so it earns its keep twice.
  • Cancellation before dispatch. Usually free; say so, and say what happens after dispatch.
These also belong on the site

The static site already renders policy pages from src/lib/policies.ts. Once the Shopify versions are written, the same text should go there so both surfaces agree — a customer who reads one thing on the product page and another at checkout is a dispute waiting to happen.

B3 — Blocker

Turn off international shipping

The store currently has an active International zone covering at least these countries:

AE  AT  AU  BE  CA  CH  CZ  DE  DK  ES
FI  FR  GB  HK  IE  IL  IT  JP  KR  …

This is Shopify's default, not something you chose. But while it is on, a customer in Germany can place and pay for an order, and you are then contractually committed to delivering a 20 kg standing desk to Germany — for a flat domestic-ish fee, with no customs paperwork, no HS codes and no country of origin on file.

Settings → Shipping and delivery → General profile → International zone → delete the zone (or remove every country from it).

Related: the weights are all zero

No product has a weight yet, and Shopify silently treats a missing weight as 0 kg. That is harmless while every rate is flat, which is the case today. It stops being harmless the moment anyone switches to carrier-calculated or weight-tiered rates — a 20 kg desk would price as if it were empty. Fill in docs/shopify/owner-input.csv before making that change.

S1 — Worth fixing

The store is called “My Store”

The store name is still Shopify's placeholder. That name is not cosmetic — it appears in the checkout header, in order confirmation and shipping emails, and in some card statement descriptors. A customer entering card details on a page headed My Store is a customer wondering whether they have been phished.

Checked again after you changed it — still “My Store”

Re-read from the Admin API, which is authoritative and uncached, and it still returns My Store. The field is Settings → General → Store details → Store name. It is easy to edit Legal business name just below it by mistake — that one is used for invoices and billing, and is a separate value.

Set Store name to HeavenCraft.

While there, check Store contact email and Sender email. Order email is already heavencraftorders@gmail.com, which is correct.

S2 — Worth fixing

Shipping rate: ₹379, and it is named in Hindi

The domestic rate is a flat ₹379, and the method is named मानक — Shopify's auto-generated Hindi for “Standard”. That name is shown to the customer at checkout, on an otherwise entirely English storefront.

Settings → Shipping and delivery → General profile → Domestic → edit the rate. Rename it to something like Standard Delivery (3–7 working days).

Is ₹379 the right number?

Worth a moment's arithmetic rather than a shrug. It is plausible for a footrest and probably optimistic for a 20 kg height-adjustable desk. Options, in increasing order of effort:

  • Keep one flat rate and accept that small items subsidise large ones. Simplest, and fine if your mix is mostly chairs.
  • Free shipping above a threshold — say ₹10,000 — with ₹379 below it. Raises average order value and is easy to explain.
  • Price-based tiers, which Shopify supports today without any product weights. This is the useful middle ground while owner-input.csv is unfilled.
  • Weight-based tiers, which are the most accurate and require the weights first. Not before then.
S3 — Worth fixing

GST registration and tax region

Prices are already set to include tax, which is the right choice for Indian retail — the number on the product page is the number the customer pays.

Two things still need confirming, and both are questions for your CA rather than for me:

GSTIN on invoices

Shopify does not issue GST-compliant invoices on its own. Most Indian stores install an invoicing app that puts the GSTIN, HSN code and the CGST/SGST split on the document. Your HSN codes go in docs/shopify/owner-input.csv.

Place of supply

Your business address and your fulfilment location are both Delhi, which is correct and means intra-Delhi orders are CGST + SGST and the rest are IGST. Verify Shopify's tax region reflects Delhi — the original plan flagged a Karnataka default.

Shipping is currently untaxed

The store is set to not charge tax on shipping. In India, delivery charges on taxable goods are generally taxable at the same rate as the goods. Worth raising with your CA before the first invoice rather than after the first audit.

S4 — Worth fixing

Place one real order

Every check on this page reads configuration. None of them proves that a customer's money reaches your bank account. Only an order does that.

  1. Create a hidden product priced at ₹1, published but not in any collection, so nobody stumbles on it.
  2. Buy it yourself from a phone, on mobile data rather than your home wifi, exactly as a customer would.
  3. Confirm all four: the order appears in Shopify; the confirmation email arrives and says HeavenCraft; the payment shows in the Razorpay dashboard; and the money is actually settled to your bank a day or two later.
  4. Then refund it — which also tests the refund path before a real customer needs it.
  5. Delete the ₹1 product.

The settlement step is the one people skip. A gateway can report success while payouts sit blocked behind an incomplete bank verification.

05 — Previewing the cart

The basket is built, and hidden

Cart and checkout are finished and deployed, behind a switch. An ordinary visitor sees exactly the site they saw yesterday — the same product pages, the same “Get a price by email” button, no basket anywhere. You can turn the basket on for your own browser and use it against the real store.

To do thisOpen this
Turn the basket ontheheavencraft.in/?cart=on
Turn it off againtheheavencraft.in/?cart=off
Straight to a producttheheavencraft.in/chairs/neuro-mesh-chair/?cart=on

It sticks. Once switched on, the basket stays on as you browse — you only need the ?cart=on once per browser. It is per-browser, so turning it on at your desk does not turn it on for anyone else, and your phone needs its own ?cart=on.

What will happen when you press Checkout

You will land on Shopify's real checkout on shop.theheavencraft.in — and then it will bounce you back to the shop homepage, because there is still no payment provider connected. That is B1, and it is the expected behaviour today rather than a bug in the cart. Everything up to that point — adding, changing quantities, removing, the running total — is live and real.

Why a query parameter and not a password

The site is a bundle of static files on GoDaddy behind Cloudflare — there is no server running our code that could check a password, which is the same constraint that made Shopify necessary for checkout in the first place. So the switch runs in your browser.

That means it keeps ordinary visitors out, not determined ones. Nobody lands on a half-finished checkout by accident, and the worst case for someone who deliberately goes looking is that they reach the same payment-less checkout you do. Worth knowing, not worth solving.
